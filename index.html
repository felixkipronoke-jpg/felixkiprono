About

Behind the data

I'm drawn to numbers the way some people are drawn to maps — always looking for the pattern that reveals something true. My work sits at the intersection of data analysis and narrative journalism: I find the signal in noisy datasets, then build stories that make that signal impossible to ignore.

What drives me is accessibility. I believe every publicly available dataset should be understandable to anyone, not just analysts. My ambition is to build tools and stories that make the world's data genuinely legible — so that a teacher in Nairobi, a policymaker in Accra, or a student in Lagos can use it to understand and shape their reality.

My career started in finance and accounting — six years managing treasury operations and bank reconciliations at MultiChoice Africa. That grounding in numbers gave me an unusual edge: I came to journalism already comfortable with messy, high-stakes data. I retrained myself in data analysis, storytelling, and multimedia production, and haven't looked back.

Today I lead data investigations at Odipo Dev / Africa Data Hub, managing a consortium of four organisations producing high-impact stories for African audiences. I've built Kenya's first living femicide database, produced award-winning health investigations, and trained journalists across the continent.
